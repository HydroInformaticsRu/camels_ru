## Why this replaces the earlier 15-cluster attribute map

The earlier draft classified catchments into 15 attribute-based clusters (Ward, all
HydroATLAS attributes). That partition had a silhouette of 0.19 and was not robust:
diagnostics show Russian catchment **attribute-space is a continuum, not a set of
discrete classes**. Specifically (Supplement diagnostics):

- the **gap statistic increases monotonically** with k (no natural cluster count);
- **HDBSCAN labels 50–80 %** of catchments as unclustered at conservative settings;
- the partition is **not robust to preprocessing** — re-scaling with a quantile
  transform changes both the silhouette (0.20) and the partition itself
  (adjusted Rand index 0.15 vs. the min-max partition).

We therefore report **density-detected end-members** rather than a forced partition.

## Methods paragraph (LaTeX-ready)

We characterise the physiographic structure of the network by detecting catchment
\emph{end-members} rather than imposing a fixed number of classes. Sixteen
physiographic and climatic HydroATLAS attributes (socioeconomic and near-empty
fields excluded) were quantile-transformed to neutralise the heavy zero-inflation of
land-cover fractions, reduced by PCA to the components explaining 90\,\% of variance,
and clustered with HDBSCAN \citep{Campello2013}. HDBSCAN extracts only
density-coherent cores and leaves low-density catchments unassigned, so the result is
a set of robust end-members plus an explicit unclassified continuum, rather than a
partition of every catchment. With \texttt{min\_cluster\_size}\,=\,40 this yields 16
end-members covering 84\,\% of catchments; the remaining 16\,\% form a continuous
core that no end-member robustly describes. End-member cores have a mean silhouette
of 0.55 and reproduce under resampling (adjusted Rand index 0.96, 20 subsamples at
85\,\%). The end-members (Table~\ref{tab:endmembers}, Fig.~\ref{fig:endmembers})
recover recognisable physiographic types — agricultural steppe, karst terrain,
lake-buffered and sandy and forested permafrost subtypes, and southern highlands —
but we stress they are descriptive anchors within a continuum, not discrete catchment
classes, and we do not use them for area-independent averaging or regionalisation.
